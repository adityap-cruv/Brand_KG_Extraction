1. Introduction to Genuin Monetize
Genuin is a short-form video experience platform that enables brands — consumer brands, retailer brands, and media brands — to embed rich video carousels and feeds directly on their websites and apps. These experiences are powered by the Genuin SDK and video infrastructure, providing a native, high-engagement environment for both organic content and advertising.
This guide is the definitive reference for activating, configuring, and managing all ad formats available on the Genuin platform. It covers both in-stream ads (which play inside the video player) and in-feed ads (which appear as cards within the scrollable feed), along with the step-by-step workflows for Genuin's Brand Control Center (BCC), the Aniview ad server, and direct sponsored content creation.

When a feed slot is identified as an ad slot, Genuin resolves which ad to show using a sequential waterfall. Higher-priority types are attempted first; the next type is tried only if the higher-priority demand returns no fill.
2. In-Feed Ads: Overview & Architecture
In-feed ads appear as discrete cards within the Genuin video feed — indistinguishable in position from regular content cards, but serving commercial purposes. Unlike in-stream ads (which interrupt an actively playing video), in-feed ads occupy defined slot positions in the scrollable feed.
Genuin's in-feed system supports both direct-sold sponsored content (served via the Genuin Video Server) and programmatic demand (served via the Aniview ad server using IMA SDK or GPT.js). When a feed slot is resolved as an ad slot, the waterfall described in Section 1.3 determines which ad type is served.

3. In-Feed Ad Types
This section provides full specifications for each of the five in-feed ad types available on the Genuin platform.
3.1 Sponsored Takeover
Description: A Sponsored Takeover converts the first ‘n’ slots of the video feed into a continuous, brand-sponsored experience. All ‘n’ slots show the sponsor's video content rather than organic feed posts. This is the highest-impact in-feed placement available on Genuin.
The Sponsored Takeover overrides all other in-feed ad types for its occupied slots. It is a direct-sold placement — there is no programmatic path for takeovers. Content is uploaded by the partner brand and served via the Genuin video server.

3.2 Sponsored Posts
Description: Sponsored Posts are individual in-feed cards containing brand-sponsored video content. Unlike the Takeover (which can claim the whole feed), Sponsored Posts follow the standard slot frequency logic — they appear at the calculated ad slot positions within the feed, interleaved with organic content.
Sponsored Posts are the primary direct-sold in-feed format. The content partner uploads the sponsored video, which is then distributed by Genuin's Video server into the appropriate feed slots.

4.3 How In-Stream Ad Serving Works
4.3.1 Prefetch Model
The player uses a non-blocking prefetch model. Ads are requested in advance of the break point, so content continues to play while the ad loads. The ad fires as soon as it is ready.
Pre-roll: ad request sent at content start
Mid-roll: ad request sent ~10–12 seconds before midpoint
Post-roll: ad request sent ~10 seconds before content ends
4.3.2 Server-Side Timeout
Each demand source has a configurable maximum response time (default 2,000 ms). This value is tunable per demand source in BCC. Experiments are recommended to find the ideal timeout — a lower timeout reduces latency but may increase unfilled breaks.
4.3.3 Skippability
The player respects the skipOffset defined in the VAST creative. After the skip offset elapses, users can either tap the skip control or swipe past the ad. Ads beyond 15 seconds must be skippable per IAB guidelines. Shorter ads may be non-skippable.
4.3.4 Dynamic Cue Points
Break positions are determined dynamically based on content video length — there are no fixed timestamps. This ensures appropriate ad placement across short-form content of varying durations.
4.4 In-Stream Ad Ops Recommendations
Use 15–30 second creatives for best performance in short-form environments.
Target ad response within 2–6 seconds for programmatic demand to reduce timeouts.
Limit VAST wrapper depth to 1–2 layers to minimise latency.
Configure server-side timeout per demand source — test at 2,000 ms and adjust based on fill rate and latency.
One ad per request — podding is not supported.
Vertical creatives (9:16) are strongly recommended for mobile-first placements.
5. Step-by-Step: Activating Monetize on Genuin
This section is the operational guide for activating each ad type on your Genuin-powered placements. Follow the two-step process: Step 1 — Configure your Monetize Preset (set the strategic parameters for which ad types to run and how often), then Step 2 — Activate each ad type following the specific workflow for Direct Sponsored Ads, In-Feed Programmatic, and In-Stream Programmatic.
5.1 Step 1 — Configure Your Monetize Preset
A Monetize Preset is the strategic control layer for all ad settings. It determines which ad types are active, their priority in the waterfall, and the frequency and behaviour parameters. Configuring your preset is the first step because it defines the envelope within which all ad types operate.

Genuin provides five read-only system presets as starting points. Choose the one that best matches your initial monetisation strategy, then copy and customise as needed.

6. Measurement & Reporting
6.1 Impression Counting
An ad impression is counted when the ad is successfully loaded and displayed — it is based on ad load, not on duration watched or user interaction. This aligns with standard IAB impression measurement definitions.
6.2 Reporting Availability
Ad impression reports are available via:
Aniview Dashboard: Granular programmatic reporting — impressions, fill rate, CPM, revenue, broken down by Channel (placement).
Genuin BCC Analytics: Aggregate ad activity for direct-sold (Sponsored Posts, Takeovers). Impression totals, engagement rates, and revenue summaries.
6.3 Ad Quality & Brand Safety
Programmatic demand is subject to Aniview's brand safety filters. Configure keyword blocklists and category exclusions in your Aniview account settings.
Vertical-only creatives (9:16) can be enforced at the Aniview channel level to maintain feed aesthetics — this is strongly recommended.
Skippable-only demand can be enforced at the request level through Aniview configuration.
Creatives can be reviewed in the Aniview creative library. Report brand safety violations to your Aniview account manager.

Term | Definition
BCC | Brand Control Center — Genuin's admin interface for partner brands to configure ad settings, manage content, and view analytics.
Aniview | Genuin's programmatic ad server (gov.aniview.com). Manages SSP connections, bid requests, and ad delivery for programmatic in-feed and in-stream ads.
IMA SDK | Google Interactive Media Ads SDK. Used by Genuin to render programmatic in-feed video ads.
GPT.js | Google Publisher Tag JavaScript library. Used by Genuin to render programmatic in-feed native and display ads.
VAST | Video Ad Serving Template. Industry standard XML format for video ad delivery. Versions 2.0, 3.0, and 4.0 are supported.
VMAP | Video Multiple Ad Playlist. XML format that enables multiple ad breaks (pre/mid/post-roll) in a single request.
Waterfall | The sequential resolution order for in-feed ad slots — higher-priority demand types are tried first; lower-priority types fill when higher ones return no demand.
Sponsored Takeover | A premium direct-sold in-feed format that replaces the first 5 feed slots with brand-sponsored video content.
Sponsored Post | A direct-sold in-feed format placing individual brand-sponsored video cards at calculated ad slot positions.
House Ad / Fallback | Genuin-served content that fills any ad slot for which all demand sources (direct and programmatic) returned no fill. Always on.
OpenRTB | Open Real-Time Bidding protocol (IAB Tech Lab). Standard format for programmatic ad auction bid requests and responses.
CPM | Cost Per Mille — ad pricing model charged per 1,000 impressions.
Fill Rate | The percentage of ad requests that result in a served ad. Low fill rate indicates insufficient demand or configuration issues.
